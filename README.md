### Abstract (English)

In the Physics of Harmony, two fundamental discrete units are distinguished:  
the **Až pixel** with energy \( E_{\text{Až}} \approx 5.876\,\mu\text{eV} \) (derived from the 21 cm hydrogen line and Landauer’s principle) and the **geometric pixel** with energy \( E_{\text{geo}} \approx 2.079\,\text{eV} \) and wavelength \( \lambda_{\text{geo}} \approx 596\,\text{nm} \) (obtained by dividing the electron soliton into \( 4096 = 2^{12} \) parts).

We show that the 596 nm wavelength is not arbitrary, but a natural resonant antenna of the solitonic network lying within the bio-quantum window of living systems:

- in the middle of the biophoton emission range from the human body (mainly 400–700 nm);
- in the main absorption band of cytochrome *c* oxidase (CcO) in the mitochondrial respiratory chain (≈590–620 nm);
- within the single-photon sensitivity range of the human eye.

Drawing on Landauer’s principle, biophoton emission and photobiomodulation effects, we argue that the 596 nm geometric pixel can act as an informational antenna, providing a channel for information exchange between the observer, the quantum vacuum and the solitonic structure at the locking temperature \( T_{\text{eq}} \approx 0.0983\,\text{K} \).

An experimental protocol is proposed to test the hypothesis, together with a summary of current experimental evidence on biophotons.
