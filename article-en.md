In the Physics of Harmony, two fundamental discrete units are distinguished:

We show that the 596 nm wavelength is not an arbitrary number, but a natural resonant antenna of the solitonic network that lies within the bio-quantum window of living systems:

- in the middle of the biophoton emission range from the human body (mainly 400–700 nm);
- in the principal absorption region of cytochrome *c* oxidase (CcO) in the mitochondrial respiratory chain (approximately 590–620 nm);
- within the single-photon sensitivity range of the human eye.

Drawing on Landauer’s principle, biophoton emission and photobiomodulation effects, we argue that the 596 nm geometric pixel can function as an informational antenna, providing a channel for information exchange between the observer, the quantum vacuum and the solitonic structure at the locking temperature \( T_{\text{eq}} \approx 0.0983\,\text{K} \).

An experimental protocol is proposed to test this hypothesis, together with a summary of the current experimental evidence on biophotons.

### 5. Indirect Experimental Evidence and the Current Status of Biophoton Research

#### 5.1 Summary of Experimental Evidence for Ultraweak Photon Emission (UPE / Biophotons)

The existence of ultraweak photon emission from living systems, as a physical phenomenon linked to metabolism, is supported by a body of independent measurements:

- **Intensity**: typically a few to a few hundred photons per square centimetre per second.
- **Spectrum**: mainly 200–800 nm. In human skin the dominant emission often lies between 450–750 nm, with a relative maximum around 570–670 nm (especially 600–650 nm).
- **Origin**: primarily reactive oxygen species (ROS), lipid peroxidation and mitochondrial processes.
- **Live versus dead**: recent studies (2024–2025) using highly sensitive cameras have shown that emission intensity from living mice is substantially higher than after death (even under temperature control).
- **Human body**: emission from the skin, particularly the hands and forehead, has been recorded repeatedly and exhibits a relatively stable anatomical pattern. Some studies also report a diurnal rhythm.

**Important limitations**:  
The extremely low intensity makes separation from background noise difficult. Claims of strong coherence or systemic regulatory roles still require further independent and reproducible confirmation. Any biological signalling or diagnostic role remains at the hypothesis stage.

#### 5.2 Other Related Evidence
- Landauer-principle experiments (Bérut et al., 2012) have confirmed the minimum energy cost of bit erasure.
- Photobiomodulation with red–orange light (590–620 nm) produces measurable biological effects via stimulation of CcO.
- Single-photon sensitivity of the human eye has been documented since the 1940s.
